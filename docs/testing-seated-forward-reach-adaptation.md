# Seated Forward Reach: support and adaptation in Testing

Direct local route: `/testing/seated-forward-reach` (add `?affected_side=left` for the left arm). This opens the existing authenticated Testing runner directly. Testing does not change assessment history, exercise plans or credits. Testing uses live measurements and displays the score without recording or saving a review video.

## Patient flow

1. Ask whether a carer or family member is present **before requesting the camera or calibrating**. Presence does not imply hands-on assistance.
2. Run the existing seated camera/lap calibration. Keep the lap anchor locked and show the selected arm, wrist, face cues and diagnostic angle arcs.
3. Start the first target immediately after calibration, without a preliminary small-movement check. The initial raised target is at calibrated shoulder height, never overhead. The initiation target stays to the screen-right of the lap at lap height. These are practical task benchmarks, not validated population norms or an instruction to force the range.
4. After 8 seconds of tracked unsuccessful effort, speak a comfortable, non-coercive encouragement. Allow another 6 tracked seconds at the same target. Only after that retry can the agent choose a reduction, announce it, then move the circle over 0.7 seconds. Contact during motion does not count. At the minimum level, another unsuccessful encouraged retry pauses for support instead of repeatedly challenging the patient.
5. Pause/stop and recording hands-on help are available throughout. Assistance applies conservatively to that step and subsequent steps. A helper should only use support techniques already shown by the patient's therapist, without pulling or forcing the arm. Assistance is explicitly confirmed because monocular pose tracking cannot reliably identify who supplied the movement.

The Testing flow uses the prepared private **Molly cloned voice** cues and matching captions. Upcoming reach and hold clips are prefetched while the initial instruction plays and reused from the cache at the target transition. A transient audio failure retries the same cue once; it never silently switches off later instructions. Repeated playback failure keeps the cue queued and displays **Resume Molly voice**, which restores playback through a browser user gesture. Calibration/target progress and adaptation remain paused until the instruction finishes or the patient explicitly turns voice off for captions. A stalled audio fetch is aborted after 15 seconds so a retry can make a new request. Stop/exit cancels pending recovery and queued speech. Camera-quality adjustment messages are shown on the calibration card and, if needed during movement, in the side controls. They do not interrupt an exercise instruction.

## Policy gradient

This integrated policy differs from the separate port-4197 experiment. It always starts each new reach at full difficulty. A tabular categorical REINFORCE policy chooses **one or two downward levels** after the encouraged retry, rather than sampling a starting height. Difficulty levels are `[1, .85, .70, .55, .40]`. There is no upward action. A final descent with only one available level is forced and does not receive a fictitious policy gradient.

State separates the task step, current level, near/far target evidence, and confirmed assistance. For raised steps, height is the difficulty fraction of the calibrated lap-to-shoulder span; X is fixed. The lap-height initiation step instead reduces its lateral distance by the conservative factor `0.65 + 0.35 × difficulty`. The stable-hold step inherits the previous reach's adapted position and difficulty. The return-to-lap anchor never changes.

Terminal reward is `2 × remaining difficulty × assistance factor` for success and **−1** for an unsuccessful terminal attempt. Assistance factor is 1 independently or 0.5 when help was confirmed. Each executed reduction costs 0.04. Reward-to-go is accumulated backward as `G = −0.04 + 0.95 × G`. Thus a completed easier attempt remains positively rewarded and an unfinished attempt is penalised more. Voluntary stopping and tracking loss do not create failure rewards; a fully observed exhausted attempt does.

For sampled action `a`, probabilities `p`, state baseline `b` and entropy `H`:

`θ_i += .08 × [(G − b)(1[i=a] − p_i) − .01 p_i(log p_i + H)]`

Then `b += .05 × (G − b)`. The baseline is taken before updating. Only executed sampled reductions receive gradients. Parameters persist in session storage for the same account, arm and browser tab, including Test again; closing the tab clears that session. No landmarks or video are stored with the policy. The report includes sampled probabilities, return and advantage so the update can be inspected.

This is a constrained learning experiment, not a clinically validated adaptive treatment policy. Reference: [OpenAI Spinning Up, policy gradients](https://spinningup.openai.com/en/latest/spinningup/rl_intro3.html).

## Movement score

The scoring references for steps 1–3 are initiation elbow extension 120°, then reach and hold elbow extension 110° plus arm elevation 50°. These references do not change when the adaptive target is lowered. Step 4 is scored only by completion of the calibrated lap target: 100 points when completed, 0 when not completed, and unscored when no step was recorded. Angle, form, difficulty and assistance deductions do not apply to that return step. Arm elevation uses model world landmarks; elbow extension uses the aspect-corrected 2D shoulder–elbow–wrist angle. Diagnostic projected arcs remain labelled separately.

For steps 1–3: `step score = (20 × target completed + 80 × mean reference attainment × difficulty) × assistance factor`. For step 4: `step score = 100 × lap target completed`.

Reference attainment is capped at 1. For Testing T1, arm elevation and elbow extension now use the **maximum valid angle anywhere within that step**, including before target contact. Returning the arm afterward does not erase the peak. At least five valid samples are still required, but the maximum itself may be one accepted sample; low-visibility or invalid frames are excluded. A confidently misplaced keypoint can still cause an erroneous peak. Each angle is maximised independently; target completion and holding remain separate requirements. The maximum and its timestamp are retained even when buffers or charts are downsampled. Return control still uses the valid-sample proportion. Testing T1 trunk lean now uses the shared `testing/trunk-lean-comparison/trunk_lean_metrics.js` shoulder-or-face detector. It takes at least eight fresh, quality-checked calibration frames spanning two seconds while the patient is instructed to sit upright, adjusts shoulder and face apparent size by hip size, and identifies a lean when the shoulder cue exceeds 12° **or** the face cue reaches 7° for at least 0.5 seconds of valid tracked effort. This replaces the prior 3D torso-vector criterion only in Testing T1. The cue estimates are not anatomical angles. Excess shoulder lift above 12° for at least 0.5 seconds is unchanged. For Testing T1, any confirmed trunk lean or excess shoulder lift during **any of the four steps** sets the **entire exercise score to exactly 15/100**, irrespective of the underlying average. This is not a per-step cap or subtraction, and even a lower average becomes 15 when compensation is confirmed. Step scores remain the earned movement/target scores for transparent explanation; return-to-lap still earns 100 for target completion. The response includes `score_before_compensation` and a `compensation_override` with the triggering steps/patterns. At least 0.5 seconds of valid evidence and a sustained threshold crossing are needed to confirm a pattern. Missing evidence alone cannot trigger the override. Standard assessment grading and other Testing tasks retain their existing formulas. Without a confirmed compensation the final score averages the four steps and requires complete evidence.

## Camera quality before starting

Testing T1 uses the existing MediaPipe Pose Landmarker Lite model with its person-segmentation output enabled. No chair object detector is used. The mask separates likely person pixels (at least 0.8) from likely background pixels (at most 0.2). In raw camera coordinates, patches below each shoulder sample the clothing and adjacent background. Their median RGB colours are converted to CIELAB; a Delta E 76 below 12 at either shoulder is treated as insufficient contrast. The message says the top **appears** to blend into the chair or background and suggests a contrasting top, chair cover or background. It does not claim to identify the chair or prove a landmark is misplaced.

At most five small 320-pixel-wide image reads per second check exposure and contrast locally. The check prefers the face for exposure so a black or white shirt is not automatically classified as dark or overexposed. Median luminance below 45/255, more than 55% very dark samples, median above 235, or more than 35% clipped samples block the start. A bright background above 170 with foreground below 85 and a ratio above 2.5 triggers backlighting guidance. These thresholds are engineering heuristics, not validated guarantees of angle accuracy. Difficult segmentation, patterns, skin tones, occlusion and unusual camera views may require further validation.

Both shoulders, both ears, both hips and the affected elbow/wrist must be in frame with visibility (and presence when provided) of at least 0.75, finite model-world coordinates, non-degenerate projected arm segments, and measurable arm elevation, elbow extension and shoulder-lift inputs. Calibration also needs the stable upright and shared face/shoulder trunk references. The lap hold, camera-quality window and posture reference are collected in parallel, rather than sequential two-second/45-frame/two-second waits. The window keeps the sample crossing its two-second boundary and at least eight fresh measurements, so lower inference frame rates cannot leave it forever just short of two seconds. The lap check tolerates an isolated wrist outlier (at least 90% inliers, with stable samples spanning two seconds) and gaps up to 450 ms. Poor lighting or a missing face/hip does not erase a separately verified lap point; sustained visible wrist displacement reacquires it. All grading checks must remain clear for two seconds; stale camera data, a tracking gap or unstable angle estimates resets the camera window. A valid posture reference and current quality checks are still required to begin, and the interface distinguishes lap progress from the camera-quality blocker. The old six-second timeout bypass is removed. The current checks are repeated after the calibration-complete instruction so a view that deteriorates during speech cannot start the exercise.

The calibration card displays six independent quality rows: lighting, clothing/background contrast, grading-landmark confidence, available angle measurements, two-second tracking stability, and upright posture reference. Each row shows Passed, Needs attention, or Waiting with its own readings or blocker. The landmark row names the affected point and visibility/presence value when below 0.75. Earlier basic visibility checks use 0.35; they do not establish grading confidence. Lighting and contrast are evaluated separately so one failure does not hide the other result. The card retains the most recent timer-reset reason and count after tracking recovers; a transient confidence dip or angle jump can therefore be distinguished from a lighting failure. Stale data is not shown as a current pass. The same acceptance thresholds and scoring rules remain in use.

During movement, bad lighting or lost grading landmarks pause measurement collection and adaptive-target timers. They do not cancel target contact if the affected wrist is still confidently tracked in fresh camera frames. Lost or stale wrist tracking pauses the target hold. Colour similarity alone is advisory when the grading landmarks remain clear. These checks do not lower the target or grade poor camera tracking as patient weakness; missing angle evidence remains unscored. The stillness requirement applies to calibration only. The camera-quality check does not upload images.

Implementation: `backend/testing_reach_camera_quality.js` contains pixel checks, landmark requirements and the readiness gate; `backend/testing_reach_flow.js` integrates it with the real runner. This feature needs real-world validation across clothing, chair colours and lighting before any claim of clinical measurement accuracy.

## Recording in Testing

Automatic review recording is disabled for all library Testing tasks, including seated forward reach and hand to mouth. Neither calibration nor movement starts a `MediaRecorder`. Completion and Stop/Exit do not save a video or companion evidence file, and the results do not display a saved-video panel. The live camera, angle overlays, movement evidence charts and scoring continue to work.

`backend/local_assessment_video.js` disables local review when `LIBRARY_TEST_MODE` is true. The standard task-history recorder already excludes that mode. Previously saved recordings are not deleted by this change.

For ideal measured criteria and form, full independent steps score 100; a 70%-difficulty step scores 76; the same assisted step scores 38. Lowering does not redefine the reference angles or award a full independent score for easier assisted completion. These weights are explicit experimental choices rather than a clinical severity grade.

The support wording follows the principle of involving carers appropriately and tailoring rehabilitation to the person's needs; see [NICE stroke rehabilitation recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng236/chapter/Recommendations). That guidance does not validate this reward or scoring formula.

## Wrist contact in Testing T1

Testing T1 uses the affected wrist from **MediaPipe Pose Landmarker Lite**. The separate Hand Landmarker is neither loaded nor run for this test. Palm and fingertip detections do not trigger its targets. Other hand tasks retain their existing hand model.

`backend/reach_target.js` selects pose landmark 15 for the left wrist or 16 for the right wrist and returns its aspect-corrected distance to the target. `testingReachWristContact` in the runner converts the target with the same `targetCanvasPoint` used for drawing, including the raw-camera lap anchor. Wrist coordinates need to be finite, inside the image and visibly tracked. Target contact, grading's at-target samples and RL near/far evidence share this distance.

The whole disk is active: distance from the affected wrist to the centre must be less than or equal to the displayed radius, including zero distance at the centre. The circle turns green immediately on contact; the spoken-instruction gate and each step's existing hold duration still apply. There is no extra movement-distance gate in Testing T1, so the hold step can continue with the wrist already raised. The T1 rim does not pulse larger than its hit radius. A valid hold also pauses the unsuccessful-attempt timer, so encouragement or lowering cannot interrupt it.

Camera quality remains checked before starting. After successful calibration, `reachCanAttempt` and `reachContactFrameValid` govern target activation separately from `reachCanMeasure` and `reachObservation`, which still protect grading and adaptation. An obscured hip or ear therefore cannot silently reject a clearly tracked wrist at the centre. Wrist visibility/presence must meet 0.5, and camera/pose frames must be no more than 250 ms old. Speech, explicit pause, incomplete calibration and a moving target still prevent hold completion. Invalid grading frames never enter the score; an attempt without enough valid measurements is reported as unscored. The side controls explain that grading is waiting while wrist contact remains available.

Verification: `node --test backend/tests/reach_target_geometry.test.cjs backend/tests/testing_reach_wrist_activation.test.cjs`. The activation regression executes the runner functions at the centre, multiple interior radii, eight directions, the boundary and just outside it for both arms and landscape/portrait geometry. It also covers wrist loss, stale camera/pose data, calibration, speech/pause gates and an RL timeout during a valid hold. The loopback-only browser test `frontend/scripts/verify-reach-wrist.cjs` exercises zero hand-model initializations, wrong-wrist/finger-only and lost-wrist rejection, centre contact with an obscured hip, interior/boundary contact, lap return, setup checks and the grading screen on desktop/right arm and portrait/left arm. It asserts zero recorder constructions, zero video-save requests and no saved-video results panel. Camera trajectories are synthetic; the runner, Molly audio and scoring endpoint are real.

Run the browser check with `QA_REACH_VOICE_TEST=1` to reject the raised-reach clip immediately after the first target (one transient rejection on desktop; two consecutive rejections followed by the resume button on portrait). Both cases check that all six normal instructions reach the audio element's real `ended` event in order, at the clip's full duration, with six cached audio requests and all four targets completed. The reach and hold clips must already have been requested during step 1.
